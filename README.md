# M2-Internship-results
Plastic pollution is a global issue impacting worldwide ecosystems and human, as well as animal,
well-being and health. The fragmentation of large plastics introduces microplastics (MP) into the
environment. Because MP sources and transport mechanisms vary considerably across regions and
ecosystems, risk assessments must be conducted at both local and global scales, accounting for the full
environmental cycle of microplastics within the Earth system. In this report, we present the results
of tap water analysis, assessing the risk of human exposure through drinking water in Toulouse, and
the results of data analysis of MP concentrations in coastal shelf sediments, evaluating the global
scale impacts of MP pollution. In Toulouse tap water we find 4594 MP/L (IQR25%−75% = 3574-6713
MP/L) within the MP size range of 1-5000 µm, the dominant polymer being polyethylene terephtha
late-PET. Our preliminary results underline the need for further investigation of human exposure to
MP through ingestion of drinking water, and identification of the PET MP source. In parallel, we use
a literature MP data compilation and machine learning to estimate the global MP stock, within the
same size range of 1-5000 µm, in coastal shelf sediments to be 67 Tg (IQR25%−75%: 5 Tg- 620 Tg).
This direct quantification of MP mass budget in coastal shelf sediments offers a framework for further
refining global mass budget models, to assess the interactions between environmental compartments.
In both parts, we underline the importance of data alignment by particle size extrapolation to enable
reliable study inter-comparisons.
